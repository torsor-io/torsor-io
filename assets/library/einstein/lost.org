* Abstract
#+BEGIN_VERSE
We take an algebraic voyage suggested by Einstein's late unease with the continuum, starting with a von Neumann algebra of observables and exploring the ways in which a spacetime structure analogous to special relativity can emerge. In particular, we identify time with modular flow (in line with Connes and Rovelli's Thermal Time Hypothesis), space with the commutative shadow of an observer-dependent subalgebra, and observers themselves with cyclic, separating states. Comparisons between observers are governed by the Connes cocycle, with the real-time cocycle near $t=0$ yielding relative entropy $S$, interpreted as a comparison between modular “clocks”, and the Radon-Nikodym point $t = -i/2$ giving a fidelity functional $\mathcal{F}$ that we interpreted as a comparison of modular “rods”. In a Type I Gibbs model, these quantities admit a closed form relation $\mathcal{F}^2 = e^{-S}$ in the well-localized (single eigenvalue) limit. We then analyze the Bisognano–Wichmann wedge, where the vacuum modular Hamiltonian generates Lorentz boosts, and discuss localized coherent excitations for which relative entropy is computable in terms of boost energy and the cocycle admits Gaussian closed forms at the Radon-Nikodym point. In this regime, special-relativistic kinematics emerges from modular data: rapidity appears as a localized relative-modular spectral parameter, while dispersion produces intrinsic “quantum” departures from rigid Lorentz scaling.
#+END_VERSE
* Introduction

#+BEGIN_VERSE
One can give good reasons why reality cannot at all be represented by a continuous field. From the quantum phenomena it appears to follow with certainty that a finite system of finite energy can be completely described by a finite set of numbers (quantum numbers). This does not seem to be in accordance with a continuum theory and must lead to an attempt to find a purely algebraic theory for the representation of reality. But nobody knows how to find the basis for such a theory.
#+END_VERSE

* Background
This section will outline standard algebraic background material,
albeit in a slightly nontraditional way. For that reason, we do not
recommend experts skip these preliminaries; they not only provide
motivation, but ground some eccentricities of presentation.
** Von Neumann algebras
A /von Neumann algebra/ is a collection $\mathfrak{M}$ of operators 
which, roughly speaking, correspond to measurements an observer can
make, actions they can perform, or equivalently, transitions between
states of the observer. This means that:
- if $a \in \mathfrak{M}$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$, then $\lambda a \in\mathfrak{M}$ since we
  can scale measurements;
- if $a, b \in \mathfrak{M}$, then $a + b \in \mathfrak{M}$ since we
  can add measurements;
- if $a, b \in \mathfrak{M}$, then $a\cdot b \in \mathfrak{M}$ since we
  can compose transformations;
- there is an antilinear operator $*: \mathfrak{M}\to \mathfrak{M}$
  satisfying $(ab)^* = b^* a^*$, with $a^*$ meaning we apply to the
  final state; and
- there is a norm $\Vert \cdot \Vert: \mathfrak{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq
  0}$ satisfying $\Vert a\Vert^2 = \lambda_\max \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$
  is a norm, where $\lambda_\max$ is the largest absolute eigenvalue
  of $a^*a$, since measurements are finite.

So far, this defines a C*-algebra (once we supplement with standard axioms,
e.g. $\mathfrak{M}$ is a vector space, the product is associative, etc, I omit).
The notion of a transition between states requires elaboration.
Usually, a state $\phi \in \mathfrak{M}^*_+$ is viewed as a linear
functional $\phi: \mathfrak{M} \to \mathbb{C}$ (written $\phi \in \mathfrak{M}^*$) which
is positive, in that $\phi(a^*a) \geq 0$ (written $\phi \in
\mathfrak{M}^*_+$) and normalized so that $\phi(e) = 1$, where $e
\in \mathfrak{M}$ is the identity. We denote Napier's constant by $\text{e}$.

Given a state, we can build a Hilbert space as follows: define the form
$\langle a, b\rangle = \phi(a^* b)$, quotient out the null space $\mathscr{N}_\phi$,
and define $\mathscr{H}_\phi$ as the vector space formed by equivalence classes
$\mathfrak{M}/\mathscr{N}_\phi$, completed with respect to the
inner product
\[
\langle \psi|\omega\rangle = \langle [a]_\phi, [b]_\phi\rangle =
\phi(a^* b) \tag{1}
\]
where the elements $\psi = [a]_\phi, \omega = [b]_\phi \in
\mathscr{H}_\phi$.
If the state $\phi$ is /faithful/, meaning that $\phi(a) = 0$ implies
$a = 0$ and hence the null space is trivial $\mathscr{N}_\phi =
\{0\}$, the induced Hilbert space $\mathscr{H}_\phi$ 
faithfully embeds $\mathfrak{M}\subseteq \mathfrak{B}(\mathscr{H})_\phi$.
We always think of a von Neumann algebra “concretely”, that is, as a
subalgebra of bounded operators acting on some Hilbert space,
$\mathfrak{M}\subseteq \mathfrak{B}(\mathscr{H})$.

We can view the inner product $\langle \psi|\omega\rangle$ as computing a /transition amplitude/
from $\omega$ to $\psi$. This gives the concrete interpretation of $a^*$ as
applying $a$ to the final state, since
\[
\langle \psi |a|\omega\rangle = \langle \psi | a\omega \rangle, \quad \langle
\psi |a^*|\omega\rangle = \langle a\psi |\omega\rangle. \tag{2}
\]
Now we come to the crux: in addition to the axioms above, a von
Neumann is closed in the /weak operator topology/,@@html:<label for="sn"

</span>@@ meaning that for
any convergent sequence of operators $a_n \to a$, the limit
\[
\langle \psi | a_n |\omega\rangle \to \langle \psi |a |\omega\rangle \tag{3}
\]
for any pair of vectors $\psi$ and $\omega$. More plainly, transition
amplitudes are a continuous function of the transition operator $a$.
Physically, this corresponds to the ability to /approximate transition

** Space
As a starting point for the representation of reality, it seems pretty
bare bones. We have no space, no time, nothing to pin the theory to
a suspect continuum; just a few axioms about observation thrown
together. Whether these are the right axioms, or some other flavour of
algebraic object, is a matter of debate. But we will see that the
structure of von Neumann algebras allows for something remarkable: the
/emergence/ of space and time.

Let's start with space.
Suppose for a moment that observables in $\mathfrak{M}$ are labelled
by location in time and space, $a(\mathbf{x}, t)$. Two observables
$a(\mathbf{x}, t), b(\mathbf{x}', t)$ sharing the same time, but in
different places, should not be able to influence each other, either
as measurements or actions; this would correspond to instantaneous
transmission of information. A robust feature of nature is that it is
/local/, i.e. no instantaneous transmission allowed. In terms of
transition amplitudes, this means that the order we apply them in
should be immaterial,
\[
\langle \psi| a(\mathbf{x}, t)b(\mathbf{x}', t)|\omega\rangle
= \langle \psi| b(\mathbf{x}', t)a(\mathbf{x}, t)|\omega\rangle \tag{4}
\]
for any initial and final states, or equivalently, $ab - ba = [a, b] =
0$. In words, $a$ and $b$ /commute/. On the other hand, if it is
possible to send a message between two spacetime points $(\mathbf{x},
t)$ and $(\mathbf{x}', t')$, and it is possible for the observer to
distinguish them, we can construct initial and final states such that
\[
\langle \psi| a(\mathbf{x}, t)b(\mathbf{x}', t')|\omega\rangle
\neq \langle \psi| b(\mathbf{x}', t')a(\mathbf{x},
t)|\omega\rangle, \tag{6}
\]
and hence $[a, b] \neq 0$. The final state simply records which
operator was observed first!

We say that points which can message each other are
/timelike-separated/ and points that cannot are
/spacelike-separated/. Operators at spacelike separated points commute.
This provides a simple algebraic recipe for space: we consider a
subalgebra $\Sigma \subseteq \mathfrak{M}$ of commuting
operators that cannot be enlarged, also called a /maximal abelian
subalgebra (masa)/.@@html:<label for="sn"

algebra we will resolve in §5.

** Observers
A lone observer makes for a dull cosmology. Introducing a second
observer corresponds to introducing a second (cyclic, separating)
state $|\omega\rangle$. The /relative modular operator/ $\Delta_{\omega|\psi}$ is
defined by
\[
\langle \omega | a^* \Delta_{\omega|\psi} b |\omega\rangle = \langle \psi|a b^* |\psi\rangle, \tag{21}
\]
so it not only swaps adjoints, but changes the reference state. As a
simple example, $\Delta_{\omega|\psi}$ converts the expectation of a
self-adjoint observable $b^*=b$ in state $|\omega\rangle$ to an expectation
in state $|\psi\rangle$:
\[
\langle \omega | \Delta_{\omega|\psi} b |\omega\rangle = \langle \psi|b |\psi\rangle. \tag{22}
\]
Again, $\Delta_{\omega|\psi}$ is positive with eigenvalues
$ \text{e}^{-\lambda}$ for bounded $\lambda$, giving a bounded /relative
modular Hamiltonian/ $K_{\omega|\psi} =
-\log\Delta_{\omega|\psi}$. Morally, $K_\psi$ captures the speed
at which operators are moving when flipped relative to $\psi$, while
$K_{\omega|\psi}$ captures how fast they are moving when flipped
between states.@@html:<label for="sn"

algebra $\mathfrak{B}(\mathscr{H})$ rather than $\mathfrak{M}$ itself,

It's a bit like timelike trajectories, which may differ in their
precise bearing, but always point into the forward lightcone.

This raises a question: if space is defined by a commuting
algebra $\Sigma$, are modular flows $\sigma^t_\psi$ “timelike”, in the
sense they point away from $\Sigma$?
More precisely, do operators generically commute with their modular
evolutions:
\[
[a, \sigma^t_\psi(a)] \overset{?}{=} 0. \tag{25}
\]
A short
calculation shows that
\[
\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t} [a, \sigma^t_\psi(a)]\big|_{t=0} = [a,
[iK_\psi, a]], \tag{26}
\]
so commuting with modular flow means commuting with the modular
Hamiltonian.
In the finite-dimensional case, $K_\psi$ is a matrix, and we can
easily build non-commuting operators $a$ which are not diagonal in the
modular energy basis.
This construction remains valid (with some bells and whistles) into
infinite dimensions, so provided the modular Hamiltonian is not
proportional to the identity $K_\psi \propto e$, modular flows are
timelike in the sense described.

By zooming in on $t=0$, we just evaluated the initial “velocity
vector” of a modular trajectory. We can perform a similar trick with
two distinct observers, $\psi$ and $\omega$, albeit with respect to
the cocycle.
If $\psi = \omega$, then
\[

\Delta^{-it}_{\psi}\Delta^{it}_\psi = e \tag{27}
\]

identity at small $t$ encodes the divergence between the flows of the
two states. To convert an operator into a scalar, we can just plug it
into one of the states, say $\omega$. Differentiating and taking $t=0$
gives the /relative entropy/
\[
S(\omega \Vert \psi) = i\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\langle\omega|
[D\omega : D\psi]_t |\omega\rangle \big|_{t=0} \tag{28}
\]
where the constant is chosen so that (by the
Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula)
\[
S(\omega \Vert \psi) = i\langle\omega|\left(i \log\Delta_{\omega|\psi} - i\log\Delta_\psi\right)|\omega\rangle.
\tag{29}\label{relent1}
\]
Assuming that $|\omega\rangle$ is normalized, the term
\[
\langle \omega| \log\Delta_\omega|\omega\rangle =
\log(\langle\omega|\Delta_\omega|\omega\rangle) =\log(\langle\omega|\omega\rangle) = 0 \tag{30}
\]
vanishes, leaving $S(\omega \Vert \psi) = -\langle \omega | \log\Delta_{\omega|\psi} |\omega\rangle$.
Substituting the real inequality $\log x \leq x - 1$, we get an operator inequality with lower bound
\[
S(\omega\Vert\psi) \geq 1 - \langle\omega |
\Delta_{\omega|\psi}|\omega \rangle = 1 - \langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 0. \tag{31}
\]
It follows that relative entropy is always non-negative.
With a little more work, one can show that $S(\omega \Vert \psi) = 0$
just in case $\omega = \psi$, so cocycle behaviour in the vicinity of
$t = 0$ precisely encodes when states are the same or different.
One again, behaviour in the vicinity of $t=0$ yields a telling
“information displacement vector” which perfectly distinguishes observers.
* Modular relativity
We now have the ingredients needed to start asking whether our
algebraic definitions of space (a masa $\Sigma \subseteq
\mathfrak{M}$), time (modular flow), and observers (cyclic, separating
states) make sense.
We will see that they can be coherently assembled into a theory of
/modular relativity/, extending the Thermal Time Hypothesis from a
single global modular flow, with reference to a mysteriously chosen
universal state, to states-as-observers with compatibly timelike but
locally different behaviour. The form of these local difference is
highly constrained and suggests ways in which special and general
relativity — Einstein's deepest contributions to physics — might emerge from the
algebraic perspective.
** Clocks
We will start by recasting the relative entropy in a suggestive
form. We have been associating modular flow to Hamiltonians of various
sorts, which interpret eigenvalues as scaling factors (for real time)

This should of course remind of something else: a clock always

analogous to time dilation, but the analogy is good enough for the moment.)

dilation — which Hamiltonian really runs faster? — is resolved by the
fact that measurements are performed /with respect to a state/. Observer
$|\omega\rangle$ measures $|\psi\rangle$ to run slower, and vice
versa, because measurement itself breaks the symmetry.

Time dilation is a cornerstone of Einstein's theory of special
relativity. The existence of a modular analogue raises three natural
questions:
- What invariance grounds this /modular time dilation/ principle, akin
  to the constancy of the speed of light?
- In special relativity, time dilation /implies/ length
  contraction. Is there a modular length contraction principle?
- Finally, how does modular time dilation relate to
  special-relativistic time dilation?
We will answer these questions in turn. The first is the simplest. The
speed of light can be measured by, say, bouncing a laser off a
mirror. The invariance of $c$ means that the result of this experiment
is the same even when we asymmetrically boost the mirror into an
inertial reference frame moving relative to us, or equivalently, we
boost ourselves.

that if we asymmetrically apply a symmetry of the vacuum, experiments
determining $c$ are invariant.

** A toy model
Consider a commutative algebra $\mathfrak{M}$ of mixed states,
diagonal in some common basis:
\[
\rho_i = \sum_{E} p_i(E) |E\rangle\langle E|. \tag{59}
\]
Since these commute, so the calculations become effectively classical.
Suppose the algebra acts on a Hilbert space $\mathscr{H}$, with
commutant (i.e. operators which commute with each element) $\mathfrak{M}'$.
The theory of finite-dimensional modular flow tells us that
\[
\Delta_{i} = \rho_i \otimes \rho_i^{-1}, \quad \Delta_{i|j} = \rho_i
\otimes \rho_j^{-1}, \quad [D\rho_i: D\rho_j]_{-i} =
\rho_i\rho_j^{-1} \otimes e. \tag{60}
\]
Thus, evaluated at a fixed energy $E$, the relative modular operator
is just the likelihood ratio, and the cocycle spectral distribution is
the log likelihood:
\[
[D\rho_i: D\rho_j]_{-i} = \frac{p_{i}(E)}{p_{j}(E)}, \quad  \tag{61}
\lambda(E) = -\log \Delta_{i|j}(E) = \log \left[\frac{p_{j}(E)}{p_{i}(E)}\right].
\]
The relative entropy is the expected log likelihood or KL divergence:
\[
S(\rho_i \Vert \rho_j) = \mathbb{E}_{j} \left[\log
\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right] = \mathbb{E}_{j} [\lambda(E)]. \tag{62}
\]
Thus, $\lambda(E)$ is the spectral density of the cocycle
Hamiltonian, $S(\rho_i\Vert\rho_j)$ is the averaged spectral density,
and treating Hamiltonian as a boost generator, the relative entropy is
the average rapidity.

\rho_j^{1/2}\rho_i^{-1/2}$, and hence
\[
\mathcal{F}(\rho_i, \rho_j)=\mbox{Tr}\big[\rho_j [D\rho_i:
D\rho_j]_{-i/2}\big] = \mbox{Tr}[\sqrt{\rho_i}\sqrt{\rho_j}] =
\sum_E \sqrt{p_i(E)p_j(E)}, \tag{63}

\frac{1}{48}\kappa_3 + \cdots, \tag{65}

claimed earlier.

* Conclusion
Einstein’s late suspicion was not merely that the continuum is “too
big,” but that it is structurally mismatched to what quantum theory
seems to demand: finite information about finite systems, encoded
algebraically rather than geometrically. In the opening vignette, we
imagined him drifting—perhaps unwillingly—toward von Neumann’s “rings
of operators,” not as a mathematician chasing abstraction, but as a
physicist chasing a language in which reality is what can be acted on
and measured. This essay took that premise seriously: start with the
algebra of operations \(\mathfrak{M}\), and ask whether the familiar
furniture of spacetime can be recovered as *derived* structure rather
than assumed background. 

What emerged is a remarkably tight triad. /Space/ appears as the
maximal commutative shadow of \(\mathfrak{M}\): a (possibly
observer-dependent) “spatial” subalgebra whose spectrum behaves like a
coordinate space, though one that naturally trades point-locality for
projection-locality. Time appears not as an external parameter but
as the intrinsic one-parameter automorphism group \(\sigma_\psi^t\)
associated to a state \(|\psi\rangle\) via Tomita–Takesaki theory: a
canonical dynamical flow that exists precisely because the algebra is
noncommutative and the state is sufficiently rich (cyclic and
separating). And finally, observers appear not as extra ingredients but as
states themselves, with relations between observers encoded by the
relative modular operator and Connes’ cocycle—the minimal “bridge”
allowing one state’s modular world to be compared to another’s.

Once one admits observers-as-states, the familiar relativity questions
can be asked in a purely information-theoretic dialect. The cocycle
evaluated near \(t=0\) produces relative entropy, which we
interpreted as an intrinsic “clock comparison”: a modular
analogue of time dilation grounded not in the invariance of \(c\), but
in the invariance of /distinguishability/ under symmetries of the
reference state. Passing from real time to the Radon–Nikodym point
\(t=-i/2\) produces fidelity, which we read as the spatial
analogue: a Jacobian-like factor converting measures on (commutative)
spatial slices, thus a modular analogue of length contraction. In
the well-localized limit these invariants lock together
(\(\mathcal{F}^2 \approx e^{-S}\)), while dispersion in the relative
modular spectrum produces a genuinely new correction term
\(\mathcal{K}\)—a quantitative reminder that “classical kinematics” is
the special case where the cocycle spectrum behaves almost like a
single rapidity.

The Rindler example shows that this is not merely metaphor. In the
wedge algebra, Bisognano–Wichmann identifies the modular Hamiltonian
of the vacuum with the Lorentz boost generator (up to \(2\pi\)), so
modular time becomes rapidity, and the “dilation/contraction” language
becomes literally geometric, i.e. diagonal in null coordinates. The
coherent-state comparison then supplies a second anchor: relative
entropy and the cocycle admit closed expressions in terms of the
one-particle boost generator and boosted smearings, making the
information-theoretic invariants computable and the localization
discussion concrete rather than rhetorical. The upshot is that
special-relativistic phenomenology is not imposed, but appears as
the geometric face of modular structure in a setting where the algebra
already knows about causal localization. 

So what becomes of Einstein’s pessimism? If the continuum is indeed
the wrong primitive, then the “purely algebraic theory” he regarded as
unknowable may not look like a discrete lattice, or a combinatorial
replacement for \(\mathbb{R}^4\), but rather like a theory in which
spacetime is the macroscopic organization of information:
commutative subalgebras as spatial surfaces, modular flows as temporal
directions, and observer-comparisons governed by invariants (relative
entropy, fidelity) that do not care about coordinates. In that light,
the sea-image from the introduction is apt: one does not break the
continuum; one quietly surrounds it, until geometry is no longer
foundational but emergent.

The open questions in §5 are therefore not epilogues but the real
beginning: what selects the “right” \(\Sigma_\psi\) for an observer;
how classical localization suppresses cumulants and Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff corrections;
how the Fisher metric on state space becomes the seed of spacetime
geometry; and whether a net of local algebras forces a
general-relativistic analogue of local Lorentz symmetry. If modular
relativity is more than a parable, its next tests will come precisely
where Einstein’s instincts were sharpest: in the tension between
locality, covariance, and thermodynamics, where “time” is not a
background parameter but something an observer extracts from the
algebra of the world.

** Editorial Preface

Albert Einstein died on April 18, 1955, in the genteel lodgings of 112
Mercer St, Princeton, where he had resided for two decades. His effects
were variously distributed: his papers went to the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, his
furniture to the Historical Society of Princeton, various odds and ends were willed to friends.
More salaciously, his brain was stolen, in broad daylight, by attending pathologist Thomas Harvey.

But Einstein's cerebellum was not the only part of his bequest to be misplaced. He kept meticulous
journals over the course of his life which, along with his writings
and scientific correspondence, were earmarked for the Hebrew University. But the processes
of delivery, catalogue, and merger were disrupted when the Jordanian government denied the university access to its Mount Scopus
campus. It then operated in over 40 locations across Jerusalem until
permanent headquarters were established in Givat Ram in 1958.

Materials for the Einstein bequest arrived in around 50 separate parcels, at
sundry times and addresses all over Jerusalem.
University president Benjamin Mazar appointed a special archival
team to oversee collection, but it was perhaps inevitable that some packages
would go missing.
Most were eventually recovered and sent to Givat Ram in 1958-59, but one package,
labeled 17B — containing letters to John von Neumann, as well as
journals from the last year of Einstein's life — remained unaccounted for.

Evidently, in 1957, package 17B made its way to the Franciscan brothers in
Rehavia, where the Hebrew University had been
leasing space. When the central archival team arrived in 1959, a distraught
registrar confessed that 17B had vanished.
The previous registrar, Brother Elgar Fuchs, had
manufactured a sudden calling to the Franciscan novitiate in Ohio,
leaving Israel in 1958 with, it seems, 17B in tow.
He may have had an Ohian buyer in mind, or Ohio may have simply been a
convenient base of operations.
In any event, 35 years later, parts of 17B resurfaced in the collection of Californian
businessman Barney Smooch.

Smooch - under the impression that the letter (from Einstein to von Neumann,
October, 1954) was legitimate - recalled the
particulars of one of Fuchs' auctions.
It seems the enterprising Fuchs had subdivided the package
into tracts of diary, then individual letters, and eventually marginal
fragments such as sketches, sentences rendered aphorism-like in
isolation, or merely samples of Einstein's handwriting.
So began the painstaking detective work of tracking down 17B.

From Smooch came Fuchs and connections to other buyers: a network of
magnates, speculators, antiquaries, and savants, spread across the
continent, many of whom had, in turn, raffled off their own portion of 17B.
The heroic details of the search have been published elsewhere [INSERT REF
HERE], but sufficed to say, it took 20 years, 15 investigators, and a
joint effort between Princeton and the Hebrew University to recover the fragments of 17B and
reconstruct it with near certitude.

The current selection from Einstein's journals (late 1954 to
early 1955) presents for the first time, and in a coherent,
self-contained form, the abrupt shift in Einstein's attitude to his
unified field theory following his declamatory postscript to the fifth edition of
/The Meaning of Relativity/. It also details some small and cautious
successes in this new line of thought. For a fuller exposition in modern
scientific language, the reader is directed to the Scientific
Afterword. For now, sit back, relax, and enjoy the newly reconstructed
17B.
** Einstein's Lost Journal
-----
*** November 1954

*November 1, 1954.* I wrote in /The Meaning of Relativity/ that

#+BEGIN_VERSE
One can give good reasons why reality cannot at all be represented by
a continuous field. From the quantum phenomena it appears to follow
with certainty that a finite system of finite energy can be completely
described by a finite set of numbers (quantum numbers). This does not
seem to be in accordance with a continuum theory and must lead to an
attempt to find a purely algebraic theory for the representation of
reality. But nobody knows how to find the basis for such a theory.
#+END_VERSE

Conversations with vN have spurred what may be a last scientific
hurrah, namely, an attempt to find this purely algebraic theory. I am
not yet feeble; like Odysseus, I yearn to follow knowledge like a
sinking star. Let us see where it leads.

*November 3, 1954.*

The problem of an approach to the continuum. If we are to start — as
the quantum theory instructs — from the observables themselves, vN
suggests that the correct object may be a ring of operators (having
renounced his lattice of projectors). I will busy myself with the
particulars later, but my understanding is that this
gives an abstract and axiomatic method of approach to the problem, but
physically represent the ability of an experimentalist to combine
observables variously and measure their size. What is most important
for our purposes is that it does not presuppose a notion of time or
space; we fix the emergence of the continuum as the sinking north star of
these preliminary investigations.

*November 7, 1954.*

vN writes of an intriguing possibility: that a notion of temporality
can be associated to a ring of operators. He calls it the "vector
group" since it is associated with an underlying choice of state
vector - as I understand it, an element of the associated
structure-preserving Hilbert space - on the ring. The question is
whether the state represents the universe, or if, as suggested by the
operational interpretation of the algebra, the configuration of an
individual experimenter and their apparatus. Details shortly.

*November 12, 1954.*

Walked with Gödel to Carnegie Lake; crisp, the autumnal glory of
Princeton on full display. Still a few boats. He outlined a
construction of a "universal" state in which measurement problems are undecidable;
this strongly favours the observer-specific notion of vector
symmetry.

*November 15, 1954.*

I suspect that, if we take the lessons of generalized
coordinate-invariance seriously, it is natural to suppose that each
experimenter $\mathscr{E}_i$ furnishes us with a specific vector state $\phi_i$
of the type suitable to generate a local notion of time. We must also determine
how to equip them with a local notion of space, since our north star
should not only be the continuum, but a spacetime continuum in accord
with the phenomenological facts.

*November 23, 1954.* 

I have been labouring over the definitions; what webs these
mathematicians weave! If we fix a ring of operators $\mathfrak{M}$,
and an observer $\phi$, vN informs me (from conversations with his
Japanese correspondent) that time emerges from the following elaborate
steps. We introduce a operator $\mathscr{T}_\phi$ with the property
that
\[
\phi(a^\dagger \mathscr{T}_\phi a) = \phi(aa^\dagger).
\]
Since $A^\dagger$ is interpreted in field theory as $A$ going
"backwards in time", we can interpret $\mathscr{T}_\phi$ as an
experimenter-relative time-reversal operator. Besides the bare
reversal, we must take into account the "velocity" of the operator
forwards in time, relative to the observer, which sits squarely in the
Heisenberg picture. This depends on the observer, rather then the
operator: the observables are all stationary but the observer is
moving. Thus, we can write $\mathscr{T}_\phi$ in terms of rapidity
as
\[
\mathscr{T}_\phi = \exp\left[-\eta_\phi\right].
\]
The time evolution operator for from vector symmetry is thus
$\mathscr{U}_t = e^{-t\eta_\phi}$.
It is very clear from this construction that we must think of time as
_emerging from the observer themselves_.

*November 29, 1954.* 

My old student, Segal, visited and pointed me to the work of Gelfand;
his cosmology is nonsense but his mathematical instincts remain
intact. The point is that a commutative sub-ring $\Sigma \subseteq
\mathfrak{M}$ should capture more
or less exactly what we mean by space, that is, the independence of
the order in which we perform experiments, irrespective of state,
which characterizes the acausality of the spacelike. My burden now is to
compute with these objects and recover, it is hoped, some reasonable
approximation of the familiar continuum. One immediate boon is that we
avoid coordinates altogether; the algebraic properties are precisely
the operational ones, so our theory is generally covariant by
construction.
